\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This article is a self-contained tutorial on the construction of
\texttt{contender\_6}, a new entry to the Coq-formalised
\emph{name-the-biggest-number} contest of
\texttt{github.com/codyroux/name-the-biggest-number}.  The previous
contender, \texttt{contender\_5}, is the largest natural number that any
closed term of System~T (simply-typed $\lambda$-calculus with
\texttt{NatRec}) of abstract-syntax-tree depth at most $42$ evaluates to.
We extend the syntax with a single new primitive, \texttt{tGrow}, whose
semantics is the function $f_{\varepsilon_0}$ from the fast-growing
hierarchy, and define \texttt{contender\_6} as the analogous depth-bounded
maximum at depth $44$ in the extended language.  The proof of strict
inequality $\texttt{contender\_5} < \texttt{contender\_6}$ proceeds by
embedding the previous contender's hidden maximiser term into the new
language, using $\texttt{tGrow}$ as one extra application layer to break
through System~T's $\varepsilon_0$ ceiling.

The tutorial presents in turn: (1)~the contest setting and the
\emph{don't-be-lazy} rule; (2)~Brouwer ordinal notations and why they
turn ordinal recursion into ordinary structural recursion; (3)~the
fast-growing hierarchy and its closure properties; (4)~the new object
language $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}$ and its denotational
semantics; (5)~a Tait-style logical relation that proves the embedding
preserves evaluation \emph{without} function extensionality; and (6)~the
final witness term and the chain of inequalities.  Every step is
accompanied by the corresponding Rocq/Coq formalisation, taken
verbatim from the inlined \texttt{Contender.v}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The contest in a nutshell}
% =======================================================================

\subsection{Big numbers, formally}

In Scott Aaronson's 1999 essay
\emph{Who Can Name the Bigger Number?}~\cite{aaronson} the rules of
the eponymous game are a single line on a blackboard: each contestant
writes a natural number, and the larger one wins.  The interesting twist
is that ``larger'' is not measured in digits or magnitude of the numeral,
but in the \emph{description} of the number.  Tower notation beats
exponential, primitive recursion beats tower, the Ackermann function
beats primitive recursion, and so on, climbing through the ordinals of
proof theory.

The Coq repository \texttt{codyroux/name-the-biggest-number} formalises
this game in a small but precise way:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Numbers must be \emph{constructive}: no busy-beaver shenanigans,
        no oracles, no inconsistent axioms.  Each contender is a closed
        term of type \texttt{nat}.
  \item Each contender comes with a \emph{formal Coq proof} that it
        strictly exceeds the previous one.
  \item Type-checking the definition takes at most $15$ seconds and
        the proof at most $60$ seconds on a reasonable machine.
  \item No axioms.
  \item The crucial \emph{don't-be-lazy} rule: the new contender
        \texttt{contender\_N} must \emph{not} be defined by literally
        invoking the previous \texttt{contender\_N-1} as a numeral.  The
        spirit of the rule is that a one-line victory of the form
        ``$\texttt{contender\_5} + 1$'' should be ineligible.  As we
        will see, this is more subtle than it sounds.
\end{itemize}

The current chain of contenders is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl@{\ }l}
\toprule
\# & Definition & Order of magnitude \\
\midrule
1 & $1$ & $\sim 1$ \\
2 & a literal numeral & several digits \\
3 & multi-tower & a few towers of $2$ \\
4 & $\texttt{ack}\ 5\ 42\ 10002$ & an Ackermannian giant \\
5 & largest closed System~T term, $\depth \leq 42$ & dwarfs every $f_\alpha$ for $\alpha < \varepsilon_0$ \\
\textbf{6} & \textbf{the present submission} & $\geq f_{\varepsilon_0}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{The previous contender, in one paragraph}

Contender~5 is the bound

\[
  \texttt{contender\_5}
  \;=\;
  \max\;\bigl\{\,\eval(t)\;:\;t \text{ a closed System~T term},\ \depth(t)\leq 42\,\bigr\}
\]

formalised by enumerating the finite set of System~T abstract syntax
trees of depth $\leq 42$, evaluating each one, and taking the maximum
via a polymorphic \texttt{maxBy} combinator.  System~T is
$\lambda$-calculus with a single base type \texttt{Nat}, function types,
and a recursor \texttt{NatRec}.  We will not need to dive into
\texttt{Contender.v}'s internals; what matters is the four facts
recorded as Coq lemmas:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item the enumerator \texttt{termsUpTo}\,$n$ is \emph{complete}:
        every closed term $t$ with $\depth(t)\leq n$ appears in it
        (\texttt{termsUpTo\_correct});
  \item \texttt{maxBy} returns either the initial best or some element
        of the list (\texttt{maxBy\_In});
  \item every element of the list is bounded above by the maximum
        (\texttt{lowerbound\_maxBy});
  \item the maximum is \emph{monotone} in the depth bound
        (\texttt{largest\_of\_depth\_monotone}).
\end{itemize}

These four lemmas suffice for everything we will do.

\subsection{Why \texttt{contender\_5+1} is not enough}

The naive way to defeat \texttt{contender\_5} is

\[
\texttt{contender\_6} \;\overset{?}{=}\; \texttt{contender\_5} + 1.
\]

This ``definition'' is morally banned by the don't-be-lazy rule: the
\emph{definition} mentions \texttt{contender\_5} directly, even though
the proof would be one line.  More subtly, \emph{any} construction
whose definitional unfolding contains the symbol
\texttt{largest\_STLCNatRec\_nat\_of\_depth} (\texttt{contender\_5}'s
search machinery) is borderline-violating the rule, and the maintainer
has historically rejected such submissions on those grounds.

Our challenge, then, is to define a number whose unfolded form does
\emph{not} mention any of \texttt{contender\_5}'s machinery, while still
proving it larger than \texttt{contender\_5}.  This is exactly the
\emph{fresh-engine} pattern.

% =======================================================================
\section{Strategy: a fresh engine for one extra layer}
% =======================================================================

\subsection{The bound on System~T}

System~T is famously bounded by the proof-theoretic ordinal of Peano
arithmetic, $\varepsilon_0$.  Concretely:

\begin{quote}\emph{Every closed System~T term $t$ of type \texttt{Nat}
satisfies $\eval(t) \leq f_\alpha(c)$ for some
$\alpha < \varepsilon_0$ and constant $c$ depending only on $t$.}
\end{quote}

So if we want a number $N > \texttt{contender\_5}$, the easiest way
\emph{not} to be lazy is to use, in the definition, a function that
\emph{cannot} be denoted by any closed System~T term: the
fast-growing-hierarchy ordinal-$\varepsilon_0$ function

\[
   \texttt{BigGrow}(n) \;\coloneqq\; f_{\varepsilon_0}(n).
\]

We will not actually \emph{prove} this bound on System~T inside
\texttt{Contender.v} -- a full proof of the connector lemma
``$\eval(t) \leq f_{\varepsilon_0}(\depth(t))$'' would be a substantial
exercise -- but we do not need it.  All we need is one explicit
\emph{lower bound} witness, and the witness we will use makes the
inequality work for purely combinatorial reasons.

\subsection{The fresh-engine pattern}

The construction is sketched in three lines:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(\arabic*)]
  \item Define a fresh object language
        $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}} = \text{System~T} \,+\, \texttt{tGrow}$,
        where \texttt{tGrow} is a unary function symbol of type
        $\texttt{Nat}\to\texttt{Nat}$ interpreted as \texttt{BigGrow}.
  \item Define
        $\texttt{contender\_6} \coloneqq \largest_{\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}}(44)$,
        the maximum of $\eval(t)$ over closed
        $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}$ terms of depth at most $44$.
  \item For the proof, retrieve the \emph{existential} witness
        $t^\star$ realising \texttt{contender\_5} (a closed
        $\depth(t^\star)\leq42$ System~T term with
        $\eval(t^\star)=\texttt{contender\_5}$, guaranteed to exist by
        \texttt{maxBy\_In}), embed it into
        $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}$ as $\embed(t^\star)$, and use the
        depth-$44$ witness term
        $\texttt{tGrow}\,(\texttt{tS}\,(\embed\,t^\star))$.
\end{enumerate}

The key observation is that the \emph{definition} of
\texttt{contender\_6} mentions only the depth-bounded enumerator,
\texttt{maxBy}, and \texttt{eval} of the new language -- no
\texttt{contender\_5} machinery whatsoever.  The proof is allowed to
use the existential maximiser, because the maintainer's rule prohibits
laziness in \emph{definitions}, not in \emph{proofs}.

% =======================================================================
\section{A primer on Brouwer ordinal notations}
% =======================================================================

The next three sections build up the function \texttt{BigGrow}.  Readers
familiar with the fast-growing hierarchy can skim them.

\subsection{The data}

Brouwer ordinal notations represent ordinals as well-founded trees:

\begin{lstlisting}
Inductive Brouwer : Type :=
| Bz    : Brouwer
| Bsucc : Brouwer -> Brouwer
| Blim  : (nat -> Brouwer) -> Brouwer.
\end{lstlisting}

The three constructors mean:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \texttt{Bz} is the ordinal $0$.
  \item \texttt{Bsucc}\,$a$ is the successor $a+1$.
  \item \texttt{Blim}\,$f$ is the limit $\sup_n f(n)$, with the
        \emph{fundamental sequence} $(f(0), f(1), \ldots)$ baked into
        the data of the term.
\end{itemize}

The fundamental-sequence-as-data presentation is the trick that makes
ordinal recursion in Coq painless: any \texttt{Fixpoint} on
\texttt{Brouwer} that recurses on a strict subterm is structurally
total, and Coq's W-type guard accepts the limit case by treating
$f(n)$, for an arbitrary fresh \texttt{n}, as a structural subterm of
\texttt{Blim}\,$f$.

\begin{remark}
This is \emph{not} an extensional presentation of ordinals: two
different fundamental sequences for the same supremum give two distinct
\texttt{Brouwer} terms.  But for fast-growing-hierarchy purposes the
choice of fundamental sequence is exactly what we want to commit to:
$f_{\omega^2}$ and $f_{\omega \cdot \omega}$ are extensionally different
on a Brouwer presentation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Embedding the natural numbers}

The natural numbers embed into Brouwer ordinals as iterated
successors:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint nat_to_B (n : nat) : Brouwer :=
  match n with
  | 0    => Bz
  | S n' => Bsucc (nat_to_B n')
  end.

Definition omega : Brouwer := Blim nat_to_B.
\end{lstlisting}

So $\omega$ is the limit of the natural-number embedding -- exactly the
ordinal-theoretic definition.

\subsection{Ordinal arithmetic}

Addition, multiplication, and $\omega$-exponentiation all proceed by
structural recursion on the second argument:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint Badd (a b : Brouwer) : Brouwer :=
  match b with
  | Bz       => a
  | Bsucc b' => Bsucc (Badd a b')
  | Blim f   => Blim (fun n => Badd a (f n))
  end.

Fixpoint Bmul (a b : Brouwer) : Brouwer :=
  match b with
  | Bz       => Bz
  | Bsucc b' => Badd (Bmul a b') a
  | Blim f   => Blim (fun n => Bmul a (f n))
  end.

Fixpoint omega_pow (a : Brouwer) : Brouwer :=
  match a with
  | Bz       => Bsucc Bz                       (* omega^0 = 1 *)
  | Bsucc a' => Bmul (omega_pow a') omega      (* omega^(a+1) = omega^a * omega *)
  | Blim f   => Blim (fun n => omega_pow (f n))
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

These mirror the textbook recursion equations for Cantor-normal-form
ordinals.  The reader is invited to convince themselves that
\texttt{Badd}, \texttt{Bmul}, and \texttt{omega\_pow} pass Coq's
positivity / guard checks: each case recurses only on a structurally
smaller argument.

\subsection{The $\omega$-tower and $\varepsilon_0$}

The $\omega$-tower iterates $\omega$-exponentiation:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint omega_tower (n : nat) : Brouwer :=
  match n with
  | 0    => Bsucc Bz
  | S n' => omega_pow (omega_tower n')
  end.

Definition epsilon_0 : Brouwer := Blim omega_tower.
\end{lstlisting}

So
\[
  \omega\!\uparrow\!\uparrow\!0 \;=\; 1,\qquad
  \omega\!\uparrow\!\uparrow\!(n{+}1) \;=\; \omega^{\,\omega\uparrow\uparrow n},\qquad
  \varepsilon_0 \;=\; \sup_n (\omega\!\uparrow\!\uparrow\!n).
\]

This is the proof-theoretic ordinal of Peano arithmetic, hence the
natural ceiling for any system at most as strong as PA.  Crucially for
us, $\varepsilon_0$ is \emph{above} the ordinal $\omega^\omega$, which is
itself above any ordinal expressible in System~T's normalisation.

% =======================================================================
\section{The fast-growing hierarchy and \texttt{BigGrow}}
% =======================================================================

\subsection{Definition}

The fast-growing hierarchy is a function indexed by ordinals:

\[
  f_0(n) = n+1,\qquad
  f_{a+1}(n) = \underbrace{f_a(f_a(\cdots f_a(n)\cdots))}_{n\text{ applications}},\qquad
  f_\lambda(n) = f_{\lambda[n]}(n),
\]

where $\lambda[n]$ is the $n$-th term of the fundamental sequence at the
limit ordinal $\lambda$.  In Coq, with our Brouwer-data presentation,
every $\lambda[n]$ is just $f(n)$ for the function $f$ stored in
\texttt{Blim}~$f$, so the limit case is one line:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint FGH (a : Brouwer) (n : nat) : nat :=
  match a with
  | Bz       => S n
  | Bsucc a' => Nat.iter n (FGH a') n
  | Blim f   => FGH (f n) n
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

Total, structurally recursive on the ordinal argument, no fuel
parameter, no \texttt{Fix}\,/\,\texttt{Acc}.

\subsection{Sanity checks at small ordinals}

For $\alpha < \omega$ we recover textbook arithmetic:
$f_0(n) = n+1$, $f_1(n) = 2n$, $f_2(n) \approx 2^n n$, $f_3(n)$ is
roughly tetrational, and so on.  The first limit ordinal already
diagonalises:
\[
  f_\omega(n) \;=\; f_{\omega[n]}(n) \;=\; f_n(n).
\]

So $f_\omega(0) = f_0(0) = 1$, $f_\omega(1) = f_1(1) = 2$,
$f_\omega(2) = f_2(2) = 8$, $f_\omega(3) = f_3(3)$ is already a number
with $\sim 10^8$ bits, and $f_\omega(4)$ is no longer printable in any
reasonable amount of memory.

For $\varepsilon_0$, we have

\[
  f_{\varepsilon_0}(n) \;=\; f_{\varepsilon_0[n]}(n)
                       \;=\; f_{\,\omega\uparrow\uparrow n}(n).
\]

A function that, on input $n$, indexes the fast-growing hierarchy at
\emph{the $n$-fold $\omega$-tower}.  This is far beyond what any
closed term of System~T can compute.

\subsection{Wrapping it up}

The function \texttt{BigGrow} is just $f_{\varepsilon_0}$:

\begin{lstlisting}
Definition BigGrow (n : nat) : nat := FGH epsilon_0 n.
\end{lstlisting}

Two crude-but-useful monotonicity facts close the section:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma Nat_iter_ge :
  forall (f : nat -> nat) (k x : nat),
    (forall n, n <= f n) ->
    x <= Nat.iter k f x.

Lemma FGH_ge : forall a n, n <= FGH a n.

Lemma BigGrow_ge : forall n, n <= BigGrow n.
Lemma BigGrow_gt_S : forall n, n < BigGrow (S n).
\end{lstlisting}

The last lemma, \texttt{BigGrow\_gt\_S}, is the only growth fact we
actually need from the entire Brouwer apparatus.  It says
\emph{`shifting up by one and feeding through \texttt{BigGrow} gives a
strictly larger value'}, and its proof is two lines: $n < n+1 \leq
\texttt{BigGrow}(n+1)$ by \texttt{FGH\_ge}.

% =======================================================================
\section{The new language $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}$}
% =======================================================================

\subsection{Syntax}

$\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}$ is System~T plus one fresh constant
\texttt{tGrow}:

\begin{lstlisting}
Inductive term :=
| tVar (x : nat)
| tLam (A B : type) (body : term)
| tApp (t1 t2 : term)
| tO
| tS
| tNatRec (R : type)
| tGrow.
\end{lstlisting}

Types are unchanged from \texttt{Contender.v}: a base type \texttt{tpNat}
plus arrow types \texttt{tpArr A B}.  Variables are de Bruijn
\emph{levels} (the convention used in \texttt{Contender.v}): index $0$
denotes the \emph{outermost} binder.

\subsection{Depth measure}

Term depth is exactly the same recursive measure as in
\texttt{Contender.v}, with one new leaf:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint term_depth (t : term) : nat :=
  match t with
  | tVar x => S (Contender.nat_depth x)
  | tLam A B body =>
      S (max (max (Contender.type_depth A) (Contender.type_depth B))
             (term_depth body))
  | tApp t1 t2 => S (max (term_depth t1) (term_depth t2))
  | tO => 1
  | tS => 1
  | tNatRec R => S (Contender.type_depth R)
  | tGrow => 1
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

\texttt{tGrow} has depth $1$, just like \texttt{tO} and \texttt{tS}: it
is a constant.  This is what lets the witness term
$\texttt{tGrow}\,(\texttt{tS}\,(\embed\,t^\star))$ stay within depth
$44$ when $\depth(t^\star) \leq 42$ (one $\texttt{tS}$ application, one
$\texttt{tGrow}$ application, each adding $1$).

\subsection{Denotational semantics}

Types are interpreted as Coq types:

\[
  \llbracket \texttt{tpNat} \rrbracket = \Nat,\qquad
  \llbracket \texttt{tpArr}\,A\,B \rrbracket =
  \llbracket A \rrbracket \to \llbracket B \rrbracket.
\]

A \emph{pack} is a dependent pair $\{A : \texttt{type} \,\&\,
\llbracket A \rrbracket\}$ -- a type tag together with a value at that
type.  Environments are lists of packs; the look-up of a de Bruijn
level returns either \texttt{Some pack} or \texttt{None}.

The interpretation function recurses structurally on the term; we use
the local aliases
$\texttt{cast}\,B$ (a partial coercion from \texttt{interp\_type from} to
\texttt{interp\_type to}, returning \texttt{error} on type mismatch) and
$\texttt{error}_{tp}$ (a default value at every type, $0$ at
\texttt{tpNat}, the constant function returning \texttt{error} at arrow
types).  Both come from \texttt{Contender.v}; we re-use them verbatim.

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint interp_term (e : list pack) (t : term) : pack :=
  match t with
  | tVar x =>
      match Contender.lookup e x with
      | Some R => R
      | None   => existT _ tpNat error
      end
  | tLam A B body =>
      existT _ (tpArr A B)
             (fun x' => cast B (projT2 (interp_term ((existT _ A x') :: e) body)))
  | tApp t1 t2 =>
      let '(existT _ R1 r1) := interp_term e t1 in
      let '(existT _ R2 r2) := interp_term e t2 in
      match R1 as R1' return interp_type R1' -> pack with
      | tpNat       => fun _  => existT _ tpNat error
      | tpArr A B   => fun r1 => existT _ B (r1 (cast A r2))
      end r1
  | tO       => existT _ tpNat 0
  | tS       => existT _ (tpArr tpNat tpNat) S
  | tNatRec R =>
      existT _ (tpArr R (tpArr (tpArr tpNat (tpArr R R)) (tpArr tpNat R)))
             (@Nat.recursion (interp_type R))
  | tGrow    => existT _ (tpArr tpNat tpNat) G.grow
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

The five System~T cases are an almost line-for-line port of
\texttt{Contender.interp\_term}.  The new case, \texttt{tGrow}, returns
the engine \texttt{G.grow} -- which we will instantiate with
\texttt{BigGrow} at the top level.

The top-level \emph{evaluator} extracts a natural number, returning $0$
when the closed term has an arrow type:

\begin{lstlisting}
Definition eval (t : term) : nat :=
  let (tp, res) := interp_term nil t in
  match tp as t0 return (interp_type t0 -> nat) with
  | tpNat => fun res : interp_type tpNat => res
  | tpArr _ _ => fun _ => 0
  end res.
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Depth-bounded enumeration and the contender}

The enumerator \texttt{termsUpTo} lists every closed term of depth
$\leq n$, segment by segment:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint termsUpTo (n : nat) : list term :=
  match n with
  | O => []
  | S m =>
    List.map tVar (Contender.natsUpTo m) ++
    List.map (fun '(A, B, body) => tLam A B body)
             (list_prod (list_prod (Contender.typesUpTo m) (Contender.typesUpTo m))
                        (termsUpTo m)) ++
    List.map (fun '(t1, t2) => tApp t1 t2)
             (list_prod (termsUpTo m) (termsUpTo m)) ++
    [tO] ++ [tS] ++
    List.map tNatRec (Contender.typesUpTo m) ++
    [tGrow]
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

Completeness of the enumerator (\emph{every} depth-bounded term is
listed) is proved as \texttt{termsUpTo\_complete}.  We reuse
\texttt{Contender.maxBy} (a polymorphic max-by-key combinator) to define
the largest evaluator, and finally:

\begin{lstlisting}
Definition largest_of_depth (n : nat) : term :=
  Contender.maxBy eval 0 tO (termsUpTo n).

Definition largest_Grow_nat_of_depth (n : nat) : nat :=
  eval (largest_of_depth n).

(* ... wrapped in Module GrowEmbed (G : GrowSig), then applied with: *)
Definition contender_6 : nat := BigGrowEmbed.contender_grow_6.
\end{lstlisting}

The unfolded form of \texttt{contender\_6}, after delta-reducing the
helpers, is

\[
  \texttt{contender\_6}
  \;=\;
  \eval_{\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}}\!\bigl(\texttt{maxBy}\,
                       \eval_{\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}}\,0\,
                       \texttt{tO}\,(\texttt{termsUpTo}\,44)\bigr).
\]

Cross-checking with \texttt{cbv beta delta [\dots] in contender\_6}
confirms that \emph{none} of \texttt{contender\_5},
\texttt{largest\_STLCNatRec\_nat\_of\_depth},
\texttt{Contender.term}, or \texttt{Contender.tNatRec} appears in the
unfolded form.  The definition is, in the technical sense intended by
the contest, \emph{clean}.

% =======================================================================
\section{The proof: a high-level map}
% =======================================================================

The strict inequality $\texttt{contender\_5} < \texttt{contender\_6}$
factors through one explicit lower bound:
\[
  \texttt{contender\_5}
  \;<\;
  \texttt{BigGrow}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1)
  \;\leq\;
  \texttt{contender\_6}.
\]

The first inequality is one line, by \texttt{BigGrow\_gt\_S}.  The
second is the heart of the proof; it splits into three sub-claims:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*),leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Existential maximiser.}  There is a closed System~T
        term $t^\star$ with $\depth(t^\star) \leq 42$ and
        $\eval(t^\star) = \texttt{contender\_5}$.  This is
        \texttt{exists\_maximizer\_42}.
  \item \textbf{Embedding preserves evaluation.}  There is an embedding
        $\embed : \texttt{Contender.term} \to \texttt{term}$ such that
        $\eval(\embed\,t) = \texttt{Contender.eval}(t)$ for every
        closed term $t$.  This is \texttt{embed\_eval}, proved via the
        logical relation.
  \item \textbf{Witness construction.}  Define
        $w(t^\star) \coloneqq
        \texttt{tApp}\,\texttt{tGrow}\,
        (\texttt{tApp}\,\texttt{tS}\,(\embed\,t^\star))$.
        Then $\depth(w(t^\star)) \leq 44$
        (\texttt{witness\_grow\_depth}) and
        $\eval(w(t^\star)) = \texttt{BigGrow}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1)$
        (\texttt{witness\_grow\_eval}).
\end{enumerate}

Combining (A), (B), (C) with \texttt{lowerbound\_maxBy} gives the
explicit bound
$\texttt{BigGrow}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1) \leq \texttt{contender\_6}$,
hence the strict inequality.

We treat each of (A), (B), (C) in turn.

% =======================================================================
\section{(A)~The existential maximiser}
% =======================================================================

The first ingredient is the inhabited witness theorem:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma exists_maximizer_42 :
  exists tstar : Contender.term,
    Contender.term_depth tstar <= 42 /\
    Contender.eval tstar = Contender.contender_5.
\end{lstlisting}

The proof is short, but it leans on a small auxiliary lemma:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma contender_5_ge_1 : 1 <= Contender.contender_5.
Proof.
  unfold Contender.contender_5, Contender.largest_STLCNatRec_nat_of_depth,
         Contender.largest_of_depth.
  change 1 with (Contender.eval (Contender.tApp Contender.tS Contender.tO)).
  apply Contender.lowerbound_maxBy
    with (x := Contender.tApp Contender.tS Contender.tO);
    [apply (proj1 (Contender.termsUpTo_correct 42 _)); cbv; lia | reflexivity].
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
The witness $\texttt{tApp}\,\texttt{tS}\,\texttt{tO}$ has depth $3$
(well within $42$), evaluates to $1$, and so by
\texttt{lowerbound\_maxBy} we get $1 \leq \texttt{contender\_5}$.

With this in hand, \texttt{exists\_maximizer\_42} is a case analysis
on \texttt{Contender.maxBy\_In}:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma exists_maximizer_42 :
  exists tstar : Contender.term,
    Contender.term_depth tstar <= 42 /\
    Contender.eval tstar = Contender.contender_5.
Proof.
  pose proof contender_5_ge_1 as Hge.
  unfold Contender.contender_5, Contender.largest_STLCNatRec_nat_of_depth,
         Contender.largest_of_depth in *.
  exists (Contender.maxBy Contender.eval 0 Contender.tO
                          (Contender.termsUpTo 42));
    split; [|reflexivity].
  destruct (Contender.maxBy_In Contender.eval (Contender.termsUpTo 42)
                               0 Contender.tO eq_refl) as [Pin | Peq].
  - apply (proj2 (Contender.termsUpTo_correct 42 _)) in Pin. exact Pin.
  - rewrite Peq in Hge. cbv in Hge. lia.
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

There are two cases: either the \texttt{maxBy} returned an element of
\texttt{termsUpTo 42} (whose depth is bounded by $42$ via
\texttt{termsUpTo\_correct}), or it returned the initial best
\texttt{tO}.  The latter case forces $\texttt{contender\_5} =
\texttt{eval}\,\texttt{tO} = 0$, contradicting
\texttt{contender\_5\_ge\_1}.

\begin{remark}
The lemma is purely an \emph{existence} statement -- there is no
algorithm here that would extract $t^\star$ in any reasonable amount
of time.  Computing the actual optimal $t^\star$ requires running the
depth-$42$ enumeration, which is astronomical.  The proof only needs
the abstract existence, and the proof script is therefore short.
\end{remark}

% =======================================================================
\section{(B)~Embedding preserves evaluation: the logical relation}
% =======================================================================

We need

\[
  \forall t \in \texttt{Contender.term},\quad
  \eval_{\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}}(\embed(t))
  \;=\;
  \texttt{Contender.eval}(t).
\]

The embedding itself is the obvious congruence map (every
constructor of \texttt{Contender.term} maps to the corresponding
constructor of $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{Grow}}$):

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint embed_term (t : Contender.term) : term :=
  match t with
  | Contender.tVar x => tVar x
  | Contender.tLam A B body => tLam A B (embed_term body)
  | Contender.tApp t1 t2 => tApp (embed_term t1) (embed_term t2)
  | Contender.tO => tO
  | Contender.tS => tS
  | Contender.tNatRec R => tNatRec R
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

Depth is obviously preserved:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma term_depth_embed : forall t,
    term_depth (embed_term t) = Contender.term_depth t.
Proof. induction t; simpl; congruence. Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Why a logical relation?}

The naive equational claim
\[
  \texttt{Contender.interp\_term}\,e_1\,t
  \;=\;
  \texttt{interp\_term}\,e_2\,(\embed\,t)
\]
fails as stated: the two interpretations have type
$\{tp : \texttt{type}\,\&\,\llbracket tp \rrbracket\}$, and at function
types two extensionally equal $\lambda$-expressions are not
\emph{intensionally} equal in Coq without function extensionality.
We do not want to introduce \texttt{FunctionalExtensionality} (it is an
axiom; the contest forbids those), so we replace propositional equality
of \emph{values} by a Tait-style logical relation that collapses to
plain equality at \texttt{tpNat}:

\begin{lstlisting}
Fixpoint RelVal (A : type) : interp_type A -> interp_type A -> Prop :=
  match A with
  | tpNat => fun x y => x = y
  | tpArr A1 A2 => fun f g => forall x y, RelVal A1 x y -> RelVal A2 (f x) (g y)
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

At \texttt{tpNat}, $\texttt{RelVal}\,x\,y \iff x=y$.  At
$\texttt{tpArr}\,A\,B$, two functions are related iff they map related
arguments to related results.  This is exactly Tait's
\emph{computability predicate}, which gives strong normalisation for
System~T; we will use it as an extensional substitute for equality.

The relation lifts to packs (a pack-level relation that asserts
\emph{both packs have the same type tag} and the values are related
at that tag) and to environments (pointwise via \texttt{Forall2}):

\begin{lstlisting}
Definition RelPack (p q : pack) : Prop :=
  exists tp (v1 v2 : interp_type tp),
    p = existT Contender.interp_type tp v1
    /\ q = existT Contender.interp_type tp v2
    /\ RelVal tp v1 v2.

Definition RelEnv (e1 e2 : list pack) : Prop :=
  List.Forall2 RelPack e1 e2.
\end{lstlisting}

A small one-line constructor \texttt{RelPack\_intro} lets us build a
\texttt{RelPack} from a pair of packs at the same tag:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma RelPack_intro :
  forall tp (v1 v2 : interp_type tp),
    RelVal tp v1 v2 ->
    RelPack (existT Contender.interp_type tp v1)
            (existT Contender.interp_type tp v2).
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Two technical facts}

Two routine lemmas about \texttt{RelVal} feed into the main
induction:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma error_related : forall tp, RelVal tp (@error tp) (@error tp).
Proof. induction tp; simpl; [reflexivity | intros _ _ _; exact IHtp2]. Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

The error value is related to itself at every type.  At \texttt{tpNat}
this is just $0 = 0$; at arrow types we use the IH on the codomain.

The second fact concerns \texttt{cast}: it preserves the relation when
applied to related arguments.  The proof is by induction on the
\texttt{from}/\texttt{to} pair; the only non-trivial case is
$\texttt{tpArr}\to\texttt{tpArr}$, where the cast wraps its argument
backwards (covariance/contravariance), so we have to prove
\emph{both} directions of the cast simultaneously:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma cast_impl_related : forall from to,
    (forall v1 v2, RelVal from v1 v2 ->
        RelVal to (fst (Contender.cast_impl from to) v1)
                  (fst (Contender.cast_impl from to) v2))
    /\
    (forall u1 u2, RelVal to u1 u2 ->
        RelVal from (snd (Contender.cast_impl from to) u1)
                    (snd (Contender.cast_impl from to) u2)).

Lemma cast_related : forall from to v1 v2,
    RelVal from v1 v2 ->
    RelVal to (@cast from to v1) (@cast from to v2).
Proof. intros; apply (proj1 (cast_impl_related _ _)); assumption. Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Look-up under related environments}

The look-up function $\texttt{Contender.lookup}\,e\,n$ has a clean
specification under related environments: \emph{either both look-ups
miss, or both succeed and return related packs}.

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma lookup_related :
  forall e1 e2 n,
    RelEnv e1 e2 ->
    match Contender.lookup e1 n, Contender.lookup e2 n with
    | Some p1, Some p2 => RelPack p1 p2
    | None, None => True
    | _, _ => False
    end.
\end{lstlisting}

The proof is a few lines: \texttt{Forall2} forces $|e_1| = |e_2|$, so
the bounds-check on \texttt{lookup} agrees on both sides, and an
auxiliary \texttt{Forall2\_nth\_error} lemma transports the
related-pack assertion through \texttt{nth\_error}.

\subsection{The main induction}

The fundamental theorem of the embedding is stated symmetrically as
``\emph{related environments map related terms}'':

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma embed_interp_related :
  forall e1 e2 t,
    RelEnv e1 e2 ->
    RelPack (Contender.interp_term e1 t)
            (interp_term e2 (embed_term t)).
\end{lstlisting}

The proof inducts on $t : \texttt{Contender.term}$.  We sketch each
case.

\paragraph{Case \texttt{tVar}.}  Both sides reduce to a \texttt{match}
on $\texttt{Contender.lookup}\,e_i\,x$; \texttt{lookup\_related} gives
exactly the four cases we need (Some/Some, Some/None, None/Some,
None/None), and the two one-sided cases are killed by
\texttt{contradiction}.  In Some/Some we use the supplied
\texttt{RelPack}; in None/None we use \texttt{RelPack\_error} (a
trivial corollary of \texttt{error\_related}).

\paragraph{Case \texttt{tLam}.}  Both sides reduce to
$\texttt{existT}\,(\texttt{tpArr}\,A\,B)\,(\lambda x'.\;\dots)$; we
apply \texttt{RelPack\_intro} at the tag $\texttt{tpArr}\,A\,B$ and
then prove the function bodies are pointwise related.  The IH gives us
that, given $\texttt{Henv}' : \texttt{RelEnv}\,(p_1::e_1)\,(p_2::e_2)$
for a related pair $(p_1,p_2)$ extending the original environments,
the bodies evaluate to a \texttt{RelPack}; we then push that through
\texttt{cast\_related}.

\paragraph{Case \texttt{tApp}.}  Both sides reduce to a \texttt{match}
on the argument-1 type tag; we use the two IHs to obtain the function
and the argument as a pair of \texttt{RelPack}s, destruct the type
tag, and discharge the \texttt{tpArr} case via the function-relation
property of \texttt{RelVal} at arrow types.  The non-arrow branches
fall to \texttt{RelPack\_error}.

\paragraph{Case \texttt{tO}.}
$\texttt{RelPack\_intro}$ at \texttt{tpNat} with witness $0=0$.

\paragraph{Case \texttt{tS}.}
$\texttt{RelPack\_intro}$ at $\texttt{tpArr}\,\texttt{tpNat}\,\texttt{tpNat}$
with the function relation
$\forall x\,y,\,x=y \Rightarrow Sx=Sy$, which is just \texttt{f\_equal}.

\paragraph{Case \texttt{tNatRec}.}  This is the most interesting case:
we need that $\texttt{Nat.recursion}$ maps related triples
(base, step, counter) to related accumulators.  A standard nested
induction on the natural-number counter -- the base case uses the
related base values, the successor case uses the step relation -- does
the job.

\subsection{From the relation to actual equality}

Because $\texttt{RelVal}\,\texttt{tpNat}\,x\,y$ is just $x=y$, the
fundamental theorem specialised to closed terms gives us \emph{exact}
equality of the natural number outputs:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma embed_eval : forall t,
    eval (embed_term t) = Contender.eval t.
Proof.
  intro t.
  pose proof (embed_interp_related [] [] t (List.Forall2_nil _))
    as (tp & v1 & v2 & Hp1 & Hp2 & Hrel).
  unfold eval, Contender.eval.
  replace (Contender.interp_term nil t) with (existT Contender.interp_type tp v1)
    by (symmetry; exact Hp1).
  replace (interp_term nil (embed_term t)) with (existT Contender.interp_type tp v2)
    by (symmetry; exact Hp2).
  destruct tp; [symmetry; exact Hrel | reflexivity].
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

The empty environment is trivially related to itself
(\texttt{Forall2\_nil}); the fundamental theorem then yields a common
type tag $tp$ and a pair of values $(v_1, v_2)$ with
$\texttt{RelVal}\,tp\,v_1\,v_2$.  At the only tag where the
top-level evaluator extracts a number -- \texttt{tpNat} --
$\texttt{RelVal}$ is plain equality, hence
$v_1=v_2$, hence the two evaluators agree.  At any other tag both
sides return $0$.

\subsection{What we did \emph{not} need}

It is worth highlighting what this proof did \emph{not} need:
\emph{function extensionality}.  At no point did we conclude
$\lambda x.\,F(x) = \lambda x.\,G(x)$ from $\forall x.\,F(x)=G(x)$.
Instead, the logical relation kept us at the relation level until we
projected at \texttt{tpNat}.  That is the crux of the no-axioms
invariant.

% =======================================================================
\section{(C)~The witness term}
% =======================================================================

\subsection{Construction}

Given a System~T term $t^\star$ -- the existential maximiser of (A) --
the witness is

\[
  w(t^\star)
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \texttt{tGrow}\,(\texttt{tS}\,(\embed\,t^\star))
\]

i.e.\ the depth-$3$ frame
$\texttt{tApp}\,\texttt{tGrow}\,(\texttt{tApp}\,\texttt{tS}\,\bullet)$
applied to $\embed\,t^\star$.

\begin{lstlisting}
Definition witness_grow (tstar : Contender.term) : term :=
  tApp tGrow (tApp tS (embed_term tstar)).
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Depth is at most $44$}

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma witness_grow_depth :
  forall tstar,
    Contender.term_depth tstar <= 42 ->
    term_depth (witness_grow tstar) <= 44.
Proof.
  intros tstar H. unfold witness_grow. simpl. rewrite term_depth_embed.
  destruct (Contender.term_depth tstar); simpl; lia.
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

The proof is by computation: \texttt{term\_depth} on the outer
\texttt{tApp tGrow} adds $1$ to the maximum of $\depth(\texttt{tGrow})
=1$ and $\depth(\texttt{tApp tS \dots})$.  The inner application adds
$1$ to $\max(\depth(\texttt{tS})=1,\,\depth(\embed\,t^\star))$.  By
\texttt{term\_depth\_embed}, this last quantity equals
$\depth(t^\star)$, which is bounded by $42$.  Working out the
arithmetic: the depth is $1 + \max(1, 1 + \max(1, \depth\,t^\star))$.
A small case split on whether $\depth\,t^\star = 0$ (impossible
because variables and constructors are at least $1$) or $\geq 1$
discharges the \texttt{Nat.max} simplifications, and \texttt{lia}
finishes.

\subsection{Eval of the witness is $\texttt{BigGrow}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1)$}

The semantic content of the witness is:

\begin{lstlisting}
Lemma witness_grow_eval :
  forall tstar,
    Contender.eval tstar = Contender.contender_5 ->
    eval (witness_grow tstar) = G.grow (S Contender.contender_5).
Proof.
  intros tstar Heq.
  assert (Hembed : eval (embed_term tstar) = Contender.contender_5)
    by (rewrite embed_eval; exact Heq).
  unfold witness_grow, eval in *.
  destruct (interp_term [] (embed_term tstar)) as [[|A B] res] eqn:E;
    simpl in Hembed.
  - subst res. cbn [interp_term]; rewrite E; cbn [interp_term]; reflexivity.
  - pose proof contender_5_ge_1; lia.
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

The argument is an extended computation.  We assume
$\texttt{Contender.eval}\,t^\star = \texttt{contender\_5}$.  By
\texttt{embed\_eval}, $\eval(\embed\,t^\star) = \texttt{contender\_5}$
too, so the closed term $\embed\,t^\star$ interprets to a pack of the
form $\texttt{existT}\,\texttt{tpNat}\,\texttt{contender\_5}$ -- in
the \texttt{tpNat} branch.  Plug this into the chain of two
\texttt{tApp}s:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,label=$\bullet$]
  \item Inner: $\texttt{interp\_term}\,[]\,(\texttt{tApp}\,\texttt{tS}\,(\embed\,t^\star))
        = \texttt{existT}\,\texttt{tpNat}\,(S\,\texttt{contender\_5})$.
  \item Outer: $\texttt{interp\_term}\,[]\,(\texttt{tGrow}\,\dots)
        = \texttt{existT}\,\texttt{tpNat}\,(\texttt{G.grow}\,(S\,\texttt{contender\_5}))$.
\end{itemize}

The final \texttt{eval} extracts the \texttt{tpNat} value, giving
exactly $\texttt{G.grow}(S\,\texttt{contender\_5})$.  Mechanically
this reduces to two \texttt{cbn [interp\_term]; rewrite E} steps.

The arrow case in the destruction is impossible: it would force
$\eval(\embed\,t^\star) = 0$, but
$\eval(\embed\,t^\star) = \texttt{contender\_5} \geq 1$.

% =======================================================================
\section{The main theorem}
% =======================================================================

We now have all three ingredients.  The first half of the chain is
\texttt{grow\_lower\_bound}:

\begin{lstlisting}
Theorem grow_lower_bound :
  G.grow (S Contender.contender_5) <= contender_grow_6.
Proof.
  unfold contender_grow_6, largest_Grow_nat_of_depth, largest_of_depth.
  destruct exists_maximizer_42 as (tstar & Hdepth & Heval).
  rewrite <- (witness_grow_eval tstar Heval).
  eapply Contender.lowerbound_maxBy with (x := witness_grow tstar). 2: reflexivity.
  apply (termsUpTo_complete 44 (witness_grow tstar)).
  apply witness_grow_depth. exact Hdepth.
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

Reading the script: we want
$\texttt{G.grow}(S\,\texttt{contender\_5}) \leq
\texttt{eval}\,(\texttt{maxBy}\,\texttt{eval}\,0\,\texttt{tO}\,
                                  (\texttt{termsUpTo}\,44))$.
We extract $t^\star$ via \texttt{exists\_maximizer\_42} and rewrite
the LHS as $\eval(w(t^\star))$ via \texttt{witness\_grow\_eval}.  Then
\texttt{lowerbound\_maxBy} with witness $w(t^\star)$ reduces the goal
to \emph{(i)~$w(t^\star)$ is in \texttt{termsUpTo 44}}, which follows
from \texttt{termsUpTo\_complete} and \texttt{witness\_grow\_depth},
and \emph{(ii)~$\eval(\texttt{tO}) = 0$}, by \texttt{reflexivity}.

The strict inequality follows immediately:

\begin{lstlisting}
Theorem contender_5_lt_contender_grow_6 :
  Contender.contender_5 < contender_grow_6.
Proof.
  eapply Nat.lt_le_trans with (m := G.grow (S Contender.contender_5)).
  - apply G.grow_gt_S.
  - exact grow_lower_bound.
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

By \texttt{G.grow\_gt\_S},
$\texttt{contender\_5} < \texttt{G.grow}(S\,\texttt{contender\_5})$;
combined with \texttt{grow\_lower\_bound} this gives strict
$<\,\texttt{contender\_grow\_6}$.

\subsection{Generic vs.\ instantiated}

The theorem inside \texttt{Module GrowEmbed (G : GrowSig)} is
parameterised over an arbitrary growth engine $G$ satisfying
$\forall n,\,n < \texttt{G.grow}(S\,n)$.  At the top level we
instantiate $G$ with \texttt{BigGrow}:

\begin{lstlisting}
Module BigGrowSig <: GrowSig.
  Definition grow := BigGrow.
  Definition grow_gt_S : forall n, n < grow (S n) := BigGrow_gt_S.
End BigGrowSig.

Module BigGrowEmbed := GrowEmbed(BigGrowSig).

Definition contender_6 : nat := BigGrowEmbed.contender_grow_6.

Theorem contender_5_lt_contender_6 : contender_5 < contender_6.
Proof. exact BigGrowEmbed.contender_5_lt_contender_grow_6. Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

The functor structure is bookkeeping: the entire proof goes through
abstractly over $G$, and the \texttt{BigGrow} specialisation is two
lines of glue.

% =======================================================================
\section{Discussion}
% =======================================================================

\subsection{Cleanliness}

The crucial fact for contest acceptance is that the unfolded
\texttt{contender\_6} does not mention \texttt{contender\_5} in any
form.  A mechanical check via
\texttt{cbv beta delta [\dots]} on the chain
\[
  \texttt{contender\_6}
  \;\rightsquigarrow\;
  \texttt{contender\_grow\_6}
  \;\rightsquigarrow\;
  \texttt{largest\_Grow\_nat\_of\_depth}\,44
  \;\rightsquigarrow\;
  \texttt{eval}\,(\texttt{Contender.maxBy}\,\texttt{eval}\,0\,\texttt{tO}\,(\texttt{termsUpTo}\,44))
\]
confirms that, after every legitimate $\delta$-reduction, the result
contains no \texttt{contender\_5}, no
\texttt{largest\_STLCNatRec\_nat\_of\_depth}, no
\texttt{Contender.term} constructor, and no other artifact of the
previous contender's machinery.  The polymorphic \texttt{maxBy} is
shared because it is a generic combinator -- it cares only about a
key function and a list, not what those things are.

The \emph{proof} of strict inequality, by contrast, freely uses the
existential maximiser of \texttt{contender\_5} (via
\texttt{maxBy\_In}), as well as Contender's \texttt{interp\_term},
\texttt{eval}, \texttt{cast\_impl}, and \texttt{lookup}.  This is
exactly the ``definitional cleanliness, proof witness reuse'' design
the contest's spirit was meant to encourage.

\subsection{How big is \texttt{contender\_6}?}

The explicit lower bound we proved is
\[
   \texttt{contender\_6}
   \;\geq\;
   \texttt{BigGrow}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1)
   \;=\;
   f_{\varepsilon_0}(\texttt{contender\_5}+1).
\]

Now \texttt{contender\_5} is itself a number that, while we cannot
compute it explicitly within any reasonable time bound, is known to
exceed $f_\alpha(42)$ for every $\alpha < \omega^\omega$ (and almost
certainly more, since System~T can encode much faster-growing
functions).  Plugging an Ackermann-scale number into a function on the
$f_{\varepsilon_0}$ scale gives a value that completely dwarfs any
contender below us in the chain, and dwarfs them by a margin that grows
faster than any \emph{System~T}-definable function in
\texttt{contender\_5}.  This is the ``decisive margin'' the contest
README hopes for.

\subsection{What about \texttt{contender\_7}?}

The fresh-engine pattern can be iterated, but each iteration faces a
gradually rising bar.  The natural next step is to replace
\texttt{BigGrow} by an even faster-growing engine: $f_{\varepsilon_\omega}$,
$f_{\zeta_0}$, $f_{\Gamma_0}$, or higher Veblen ordinals.  All of
these can be presented in the Brouwer style in Coq, but the
\texttt{Brouwer} type itself does not enforce well-foundedness
extensionally, so writing down the right \emph{fundamental sequence}
becomes the active design question rather than the recursion structure.

A second axis is the \emph{connector lemma}: an actual proof that
every closed System~T term of depth $d$ is bounded by
$f_\alpha(d)$ for some $\alpha < \varepsilon_0$.  Such a lemma would
let us drop the embedding altogether and write
$\texttt{contender\_N} \coloneqq \texttt{BigGrow}\,N$ for some $N$.
This is the cleanest possible submission shape, but the proof is a
significant exercise in proof-theoretic ordinal analysis and is well
out of scope here.

\subsection{Summary of dependencies}

The whole submission is self-contained within \texttt{Contender.v} and
introduces no external libraries beyond \texttt{Arith}, \texttt{Lia},
and \texttt{List} (already required by the file).  No
\texttt{FunctionalExtensionality}, no \texttt{ProofIrrelevance},
no \texttt{ClassicalLogic}.  Both
\texttt{Print Assumptions contender\_4\_lt\_contender\_5} and
\texttt{Print Assumptions contender\_5\_lt\_contender\_6} report
